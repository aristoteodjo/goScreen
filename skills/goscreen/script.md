# Script

What is said and what is shown are two tracks of one story. Write the script
first, in the site's language (`locale`), agree on it with the person, then
build the montage to it.

## One idea

- One idea per video. If a second idea appears, it is another video or the
  outro's teaser.
- Structure of a product film: **hook** (the problem or the promise) →
  **product** on screen, with one figure of proof → **solution** in use →
  **2 or 3 features**, each with a figure → **call to action**, very short.
- An explainer adds a "why it matters" beat between the hook and the product;
  a proof clip is hook → three figures → call to action; a VSL keeps one
  benefit per scene, in the order of the journey.

## Length

- Spoken rate: about 2.3 words per second (140 words a minute). Length in
  seconds = words ÷ 2.3. A 60 to 80 word script lasts about 30 s.
- Ranges: ad 9:16 15 to 30 s; proof clip 1:1 15 to 30 s; teaser 15 to 30 s;
  launch 16:9 45 to 70 s; demo or walkthrough 45 to 90 s; explainer 60 to
  120 s; VSL 30 to 90 s.
- One phrase per scene; the cut lands on the attack of the next phrase
  (`edit.alignToVoice`). No spoken line longer than 4 s. Scenes of a filmed
  site: 4 to 10 s.

## Hook

- Spoken hook done by 3 s. First visual change before 1 s; the first beat
  change (new element, new shot) before 5 s.
- On-screen hook ≤ 6 words, one accent word, not the same words as the
  spoken hook: the screen completes the voice, it does not subtitle it.
- Hook forms that work: the pain in the viewer's words ("Still copying
  invoices by hand?"), the result first ("3 days faster, every month"), the
  contrast ("One inbox. Every supplier."). It gets the most work: a mini
  story in two or three acts, layered (text, shape, zoom), an impact on each
  key word.
- Banned openings: a greeting, the brand name, "in this video", "did you
  know", a question that the viewer can answer "no" to and leave.

## Quality gates

Read the script against each gate. One failed gate means rewriting, not
patching.

1. **Generic**: could this script be about any product? Then it names
   nothing real. Put the product's actual words, screens and numbers in it.
2. **Shapeless**: can you name the hook, the turn and the call to action by
   line? If not, there is no structure.
3. **Interchangeable**: would swapping two scenes change nothing? Then there
   is no progression. Each scene must need the previous one.
4. **"Viral"**: does it rely on a trick (countdown, fake urgency, a claim
   with no figure)? Replace the trick with a proof.

## Captions and subtitles

- Captions (scene titles) say the benefit, 3 to 6 words, one accent word,
  figures accented. They are not the voice's transcript.
- Subtitles, when wanted, show 1 to 3 words at a time on the beat of the
  voice, inside the bottom safe zone (9:16: above `y` 0.78).
- The call to action is one verb and one object ("Start free", "Book a
  demo"), the address under it, on screen for at least 2 s.

## Numbers

- Every figure said is shown (`stat-counter`), with its unit and context
  ("2 184 trays today"). Never a figure without a source the person
  confirmed.
- Prefer one strong figure per scene to three weak ones.
